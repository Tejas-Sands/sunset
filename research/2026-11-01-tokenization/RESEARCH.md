# SUNSET tokenization decision research — 1 November 2026

**Recommendation: implement at most one transferable, nonfinancial stewardship NFT for each successfully released pack, with a narrow ability to maintain a preferred manifest mirror for the already committed public artifact. Do not tokenize the copyright, existing escrow or creator compensation.** Keep a sponsorship receipt as ordinary verifiable activity. Defer prepaid service vouchers until there is an actual provider and a complete service-redemption workflow.

This is a product and standards assessment for the tokenization implementation batch. The coordinating implementer selected a single manifest-mirror URI, full NEP-171/177/181 interfaces, exact schema-2 manifests, and resetting the mirror on every actual ownership change. These are implementation decisions, not claims of completed verification. This report does not establish deployment, protocol execution, user demand, legal rights ownership or hackathon acceptance. No deployment, provisioning, transaction signing or external messaging was performed for this research.

## Existing product and constraint

The current [product README](../../projects/sunset/README.md), [build ledger](../../projects/sunset/docs/BUILD_LEDGER.md), [council decision](../2026-11-01-council/README.md), [contract ABI](../../projects/sunset/contracts/sunset/README.md) and source define capped adult sponsorship of one committed resource. Publication of the matching key before expiry credits the fixed creator; failed funding or release permits the contributor's specified refund. The browser's role-play accounts are not authenticated identities. Local activity and CSV exports are not transaction receipts.

The [pack validator](../../projects/sunset/src/lib/pack.mjs) accepts only original-format `CC0-1.0` packs. The [artifact producer](../../projects/sunset/src/lib/demo.mjs) binds exact manifest bytes, ciphertext, plaintext and release key. [Independent recovery](../../projects/sunset/scripts/recover-pack.mjs) already verifies retained files without a server or wallet. The remaining availability problem is finding a retained authentic copy when the original host disappears. A token could assign the maintenance of a public directory; it cannot make an unavailable copy exist.

Preserve these invariants:

- A learner can use and keep the released resource without holding a token, connecting a wallet, contacting SUNSET or paying again.
- Creator, target, deadlines, reviewer approval, committed bytes, license and existing release/refund/withdrawal accounting stay fixed.
- Token transfer changes only a clearly described maintenance role. It never moves campaign credit, contribution records, refund claims, creator identity or release authority.
- Anyone may independently copy, host and share the CC0 work. The token holder controls the contract's preferred manifest-mirror URI, not the public's right to mirror it.

## Four options compared

| Option | Actual job it could do | Fit for this SUNSET | Decision |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Tokenized copyright or revenue shares | Represent an expressly defined assignment, or entitlement to a separately defined stream of money | The released pack is CC0 and creator compensation is already a capped payment to a fixed beneficiary. There is no existing post-release revenue stream to divide. Adding one changes the commercial agreement, accounting, recovery and claims the product must substantiate. | Reject for this batch. A token is not evidence that the minter owns rights. |
| Transferable prepaid service voucher | Let an institution give another institution a finite entitlement to a specified provider's tutoring, translation, printing or hosting work | This could benefit real users when the provider, units, expiry, proof of service, cancellation and refund beneficiaries are explicit. It must remain a separate service; existing CC0 copies cannot expire. The current service grants are local rehearsals outside the Wasm. | Defer. Build only with a real service workflow; do not mint nominal “access” to already public content. |
| Transferable stewardship NFT | Hand responsibility for a released pack's preferred public mirror directory to a successor without changing the artifact | This addresses a problem the current product has: its public commitment survives a dead host, but artifact discovery may not. Current-holder authorization gives transfer an observable consequence. It fits the product only while mirrors remain optional and bytes are independently verified. | Smallest useful tokenization candidate. One token per released campaign; no sale, treasury, revenue sharing or extra entitlement system. |
| Sponsorship receipt | Prove that a specific account contributed to a campaign and show its outcome | Already represented by immutable campaign/contribution facts plus actual transaction outcomes once available. Transferring a receipt cannot make the recipient the original sponsor; a wallet collectible adds no settlement guarantee. | Keep an account-bound receipt/activity view. Do not add a token solely for a badge or another track. |

These judgments are product inferences from the existing SUNSET design, not findings of user research. A named account with a transferable maintainer field could implement the same authority with less interoperability work. The NFT is justified only if exposing a standard transferable ownership object is a deliberate product goal and the complete standard is implemented. The maintenance capability, rather than its image or scarcity, must carry the demonstration.

### Rights: what a token can and cannot mean here

Creative Commons explicitly distinguishes token ownership from copyright ownership: an NFT purchase alone does not assign the linked work's copyright. Its NFT FAQ also confirms that a token can be transferred while the underlying CC0 work remains freely reusable by everyone. A SUNSET token therefore must say “steward of the public mirror directory,” never “owns this resource,” “owns the copyright,” or “exclusive learning access.” [Creative Commons NFT FAQ](https://creativecommons.org/cc-and-nfts/).

CC0 attempts an irrevocable waiver with a public-license fallback where necessary. It does not create assurances that the affirmer actually controlled every relevant right, and patent, trademark and third-party rights remain outside its stated reach. SUNSET's independent content review remains necessary; a hash or mint is no replacement. Original sample content is a sound demonstration boundary. [CC0 legal code](https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/zero/1.0/legalcode.en), [CC0 deed](https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/zero/1.0/).

The royalty standard does not solve this mismatch. NEP-199 defines `nft_payout` and `nft_transfer_payout` so a financial application can query and validate an NFT-sale payout map. It does not manufacture copyright ownership, guarantee a buyer, or create revenues from use of a CC0 pack. It also introduces marketplace/approval and payout behavior absent from the recommended feature. [NEP-199](https://github.com/near/NEPs/blob/master/neps/nep-0199.md).

### Why a voucher is a different product feature

A useful voucher needs a provider promise that remains understandable when transferred: exact service, remaining units, who may redeem, unit pricing, provider acceptance, expiry, the party entitled to refunds, and whether an unfinished service can be reassigned. Transfer and redemption must not both consume the same entitlement. Transferring the right to receive future work must not transfer the original sponsor's campaign refund by accident.

Identical units from one provider under identical terms could use fungible balances. Different providers, expiry dates and service promises are not automatically interchangeable; a single voucher object or existing bounded service-grant record is easier to reason about than a general coin. NEP-141 standardizes transfer, transfer-and-call, supply and account balances; it leaves service delivery and redemption semantics to the application. Transfer calls require receiver handling and a private resolver, so an FT does not remove asynchronous settlement work. Amounts are decimal strings for unsigned 128-bit values. [NEP-141](https://github.com/near/NEPs/blob/master/neps/nep-0141.md).

FT ergonomics also involve storage registration and metadata: NEP-145 defines storage deposit/withdrawal/unregistration and balance views; NEP-148 defines `ft_metadata`. Those are costs to justify with a service use case, not evidence that a voucher is needed today. [NEP-145](https://github.com/near/NEPs/blob/master/neps/nep-0145.md), [NEP-148](https://github.com/near/NEPs/blob/master/neps/nep-0148.md).

## Minimum stewardship behavior

This is a proposed implementation boundary, not a claim about the current contract.

1. **One released version, one token.** Use the campaign ID as the token ID within the actual contract account. Before successful release, `nft_token` returns null and no authority exists. On successful release, mint exactly once to the fixed creator. The financial release still credits only that creator. A failed/refundable campaign never acquires a token.
2. **Keep immutable identity separate from mutable discovery.** Campaign hashes, agreement digest, original manifest URI, license and public release key retain their original meaning. A separate, single preferred manifest-mirror URI can change. A location must serve the exact committed manifest bytes; changes to questions or runtime are a new artifact/version, not maintenance of this one. The selected schema-2 manifest commits `ciphertext_path: "ciphertext.sunset"`, allowing the exact manifest and ciphertext to move together. This schema belongs in newly prepared agreements; do not silently reinterpret or rewrite existing schema-1 commitments.
3. **One concrete privilege.** The current owner can replace that public HTTPS URI within the existing 512-byte bound. Every actual ownership change, including resolver return to the previous owner, resets it to the immutable original manifest URI. Previous owners cannot update it after transfer. Nobody needs ownership to read the URI, verify a local copy, or host another copy. A transfer is a handover, not an endorsement by the creator.
4. **Verify at the consumer.** The contract can validate URL syntax and bounds; it cannot fetch a URL or prove it hosts authentic, available bytes. The client/recovery tool must check exact manifest bytes against the immutable manifest hash before resolving the strictly validated relative ciphertext path. It must then verify ciphertext hash, key commitment, authenticated decryption, plaintext hash and CC0 pack identity. Show “preferred mirror” until that specific retrieval has passed verification. Never label an owner's URL itself “verified.”
5. **Keep the learner journey independent.** Mirror retrieval accepts only bounded data and the existing validated pack format; it must never execute remote HTML or scripts. Treat owner-supplied URLs as untrusted, omit credentials, reject unsafe schemes/credentials and avoid automatic navigation. Prefer explicit retrieval and local file recovery. Learner play and downloaded copies remain offline.
6. **Fund the storage before release.** Reserve bounded space for the eventual owner, mirror URI and transfer-call requirements during creation, paid separately from campaign economics. Handle transfers from shorter to longer valid account IDs and replacement URLs without unfunded growth. A callback or release must not allocate an uncharged recipient or require the creator's escrow for storage.
7. **Keep token security deposits separate.** Any required one-yocto transfer deposits are not sponsorship and must not enlarge the campaign cap, creator payout, contributor credit or refund. Record/disclose them as separate retained security deposits or otherwise make their exclusion explicit in the accounting invariant. Token owners need not become registered contributors merely to hold a maintenance role.
8. **Retain an inspectable history.** Mint, transfer and relevant metadata updates emit standardized events; mirror changes also expose bounded current state and a useful custom event. No private learner data belongs in metadata, URLs or events.

The word “canonical” needs care: it can describe the directory recognized by this contract, not a monopoly on authentic copies. A mirror of identical committed bytes is authentic even when the current steward did not list it. A hostile or absent holder can damage this directory's usefulness, but must not invalidate retained copies or prohibit independent discovery.

### Explicit limits

- Loss of the holder's signing key can strand the maintenance role. The proposed minimal feature supplies no administrative seizure or account-recovery promise. Copies and permission to host them survive.
- A holder may publish dead or malicious locations. Byte verification prevents accepted content substitution; it cannot guarantee uptime, affordability, honest metadata or someone continuing to host the data.
- A public-looking DNS hostname is not proof of a public network destination and is not DNS-rebinding protection. Keep retrieval an explicit browser action without credentials or a server proxy; do not advertise the syntax filter as network isolation.
- HTTPS hosting is sufficient for the minimum feature. Adding IPFS is optional; a CID changes with the content, while continued availability still requires retained/pinned copies. An NFT or content address is not a storage contract. [IPFS immutability](https://docs.ipfs.tech/concepts/immutability/), [IPFS persistence](https://docs.ipfs.tech/concepts/persistence/).
- The current development contract remains upgradeable while full-access control exists. Adding a token does not make the contract immutable or audit its existing money handling.
- No new financial promises, market, resale interface, treasury, token-bound account, staking or yield are needed. This research does not assess securities or other jurisdiction-specific treatment.

## NEAR interoperability requirements

Do not describe an owner field plus a custom transfer method as a completed NEP-171 NFT. The following interfaces and failure behavior determine the minimum credible claim.

| Standard | Requirements relevant to this feature |
| --- | --- |
| [NEP-171 core](https://github.com/near/NEPs/blob/master/neps/nep-0171.md) | Provide `nft_transfer(receiver_id, token_id, approval_id?, memo?)`, `nft_transfer_call(receiver_id, token_id, approval_id?, memo?, msg)`, and `nft_token(token_id) -> Token \| null`. Token includes `token_id` and `owner_id`. Transfers require a one-yocto deposit and owner/approved-caller authorization. The transfer-call chain invokes `nft_on_transfer`; its self-only resolver must handle failure/return requests. Approvals are optional; do not imply support when omitted. Core specifies NFT events using `nep171`, version `1.2.0`. |
| [NEP-177 metadata](https://github.com/near/NEPs/blob/master/neps/nep-0177.md) | Provide `nft_metadata()` and token `metadata`. Contract metadata uses `spec: "nft-1.0.0"`, name and symbol, with the specified nullable fields. `reference_hash` and `media_hash` are **base64-encoded SHA-256**, required when their corresponding URLs are present; existing SUNSET hex hashes must be converted, not copied into those fields. Keep optional remote metadata out unless it has an actual retained committed file. Clearly describe the maintenance authority in metadata. |
| [NEP-181 enumeration](https://github.com/near/NEPs/blob/master/neps/nep-0181.md) | Optional extension useful for wallet discovery: `nft_total_supply`, `nft_tokens`, `nft_supply_for_owner`, `nft_tokens_for_owner`. Supply counts are decimal strings; list calls support the specified pagination. At five campaigns the implementation can be bounded without a separate indexer. |
| [NEP-297 events](https://github.com/near/NEPs/blob/master/neps/nep-0297.md) | Emit one `EVENT_JSON:` JSON object per log with `standard`, `version`, `event`, and relevant `data`. Serialize JSON rather than interpolating user strings into logs. NFT event payloads must follow NEP-171; application mirror events use a distinct documented application standard. |
| [NEP-178 approvals](https://github.com/near/NEPs/blob/master/neps/nep-0178.md) | Optional. Omit delegated transfer permissions and sale integrations in this batch. Adding them later requires approval IDs and clearing/restoring approvals correctly around transfers. |

A subtle application-level failure matters here: a receiver contract provisionally owns an NFT during `nft_transfer_call`. If it can change its mirror and then reject the transfer, reverting ownership alone would leave the receiver's mirror update behind. The selected product rule resets the preferred URI to the immutable original on **every actual ownership change**, including resolver return. It permits current-owner maintenance during a transfer call and removes the receiver's persistent mirror state if ownership returns. This avoids a global pending-operation lock whose callback failure could strand maintenance. A client must still verify bytes during any provisional ownership period.

This reset rule is SUNSET-specific behavior inferred from asynchronous execution; it is not a requirement claimed to appear in NEP-171. An accepted transfer leaves the accepted holder's subsequent updates in place. A receiver that forwards the NFT triggers another reset; the later valid holder can then maintain it. A resolver that observes a forwarded NFT and makes no ownership change must leave that later holder's updates alone. No external financial or access side effects are attached to mirror changes.

Follow the reference resolver's treatment of malformed/failed results and current ownership: a rejection cannot claw the NFT back from a third party if the receiver already forwarded it. Private callbacks must authenticate their self-only receipt context and actual promise result, then check current ownership rather than trusting arbitrary public input. Any added operation identity must also be validated. [NEAR reference NFT resolver](https://github.com/near/near-sdk-rs/blob/master/near-contract-standards/src/non_fungible_token/core/core_impl.rs).

A method that always rejects `nft_transfer_call` must not be presented as full core support. The chosen reset rule is intended to preserve the reference transfer path rather than impose a pending-transfer lock.

## Track fit, novelty and proof

The official track is **NFTs That Do Something**, ID `nfts`. Its public description calls for ownership with behavior and gives assets with accounts, inventory, credentials, memberships and access rights as examples. The official example uses a transferable account and explicitly asks the builder to explain how control passes and why a seller loses access. SUNSET's narrow mirror-maintenance authority is an analogous concrete capability, not the account/treasury example itself. [Official event](https://nearhackathon.com/), [official NFT example](https://github.com/nearai/near-hackathon-prompt-examples/blob/main/tracks/10-nfts.md).

Live rules Q-010 allow testnet-only projects; Q-011 says named integrations and prompt examples are hints. A useful own contract can fit without sub-accounts or a token treasury. Local-only demos are permitted under Q-012 with a reproducible public repo and video; this permission does not establish that simulated ownership is an actual NEAR token. Recheck rules before submission. [Official live rules](https://nearhackathon.com/HACKATHON.md).

**Assessment:** stewardship provides a plausible secondary NFT-track case after the behavior works. A receipt collectible gives a weak case. Vouchers could give a stronger direct entitlement case later, but are a separate unimplemented service system. Copyright tokens do not improve the current mechanism. Track eligibility is an organizer decision; this report does not claim qualification, prize stacking or a scoring benefit from more tracks.

**Novelty:** transferable authorization, dynamic NFT metadata, public-domain works with NFTs, and immutable-content/mutable-discovery designs already exist. The official example itself demonstrates transferable control. Claim no invention of these primitives or of public buyouts. The useful SUNSET combination is a sponsored, exactly committed CC0 release whose reference-maintenance role can pass to a successor while everyone retains independent access. This research finds a coherent implementation story, not proven novelty or demand.

### Required evidence before describing the feature as complete

1. Release once and inspect the minted token, immutable campaign fields, creator credit and absence of token on both refund paths.
2. Transfer from creator A to steward B. The mirror resets to the original URI. B can replace it; A and unrelated C are rejected. Existing creator/contributor balances and identities remain identical.
3. Make the original host unavailable. Fetch a retained matching artifact from B's listed location, verify every commitment, recover and play the standalone game with networking disabled. A corrupted or substituted mirror must fail before any remote content runs. A retained unlisted authentic copy must still recover.
4. Exercise the actual transfer-call outcomes: receiver accepts, rejects, panics, returns malformed/oversized data, and forwards the token. Test forged/stale callback attempts. A provisional receiver's mirror update must reset on return, while a later forwarded holder's legitimate update survives a resolver that makes no ownership change. Distinguish host doubles from protocol receipt/gas tests.
5. Check storage using maximum valid owner lengths and maximum mirror URI bytes. Show that release, rollback and callback do not consume campaign escrow for storage, and one-yocto security deposits do not enter financial credits.
6. Check metadata/event encoding and bounded enumeration against the actual ABI. Record browser and protocol results separately. Native tests and compiled-Wasm host doubles alone do not prove real NEAR transfer-call receipts or wallet interoperability.

Until these gates pass, position Open Innovation and Social Money as the established product directions, describe tokenization as a local implementation or proposed extension at the level actually evidenced, and keep live testnet claims conditional. The strongest next user validation is a creator handing maintenance to an independent teacher/library and both explaining why the handover helps them.

### Independent client review during implementation

Read-only review covered `mirror.mjs`, `stewardship.mjs`, the artifact producer, campaign preparation, recovery and their focused tests. It found a trailing-dot local-hostname bypass and inconsistent original-manifest URI validation in preparation. The implementer fixed both by rejecting trailing-dot hosts, checking normalized UTF-8 URL length, and using the shared validator when preparing agreements. Independent rerun passed eight mirror/stewardship/preparation tests; direct probes rejected the three previously accepted local-name variants. This is a focused review, not a security audit or browser/protocol proof.

The subsequent integration now accepts an optional `licenseHash` commitment. The configured-contract public-resource viewer supplies the actual onchain `license_hash`, which must equal SHA-256 of the verified manifest’s CC0 license before any ciphertext fetch. A regression test rejects an inconsistent license commitment. Local retained-file recovery still relies on exact manifest/plaintext commitments and its strict CC0 pack validator; it does not invent an onchain commitment.
