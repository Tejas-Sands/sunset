# New ownership and collective action proposals

**SUNSET is the strongest new direction from this round:** an agreement that pays a creator to turn a specified commercial digital asset into a public resource. **MOLT is the alternative:** a way for a community to form a funded alternative together, with different participants setting different conditions for joining.

The goal is an unusual, consequential project with a visible economic change in its demo. NEAR should enforce an agreement that participants have a reason to care about. A collection of AI chat interfaces would not establish either idea.

| Proposal | Question | What the demo must establish | Main weakness |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [SUNSET](sunset.md) | Can a creator get paid while committing to end a product's access restrictions? | Paid use reaches a fixed buyout cap; the exact committed asset is released; an unrelated person uses it | Revenue-triggered openness has substantial prior art; unattended key release remains a technical dependency |
| [MOLT](molt.md) | Can a community form a viable alternative without asking someone to fund an unsuccessful launch? | A mutually acceptable coalition forms from different conditions; an unsuccessful round refunds; a stale proposal cannot spend | Conditional commitments already exist; a community must actually want to move |

These began as research proposals, not implemented products or claims of world-first inventions. **The user subsequently selected SUNSET for the build.** Its [maintained architecture](../../projects/sunset/README.md) covers the free Vercel, Supabase and NEAR testnet deployment. MOLT remains a research candidate. Working names have not been checked for conflicts.

LIFEBOAT is now retained in [the main project directory](../../projects/lifeboat/README.md), alongside COMMONS. It remains a separate continuity project; SUNSET and MOLT do not require implementing it first.

## What would change in the world

SUNSET could let purchases finance the permanent release of useful software, original teaching material, specialist data or appropriately licensed model assets. Creators receive a defined payment; later users inherit a reusable resource. Hosting, maintenance and computation still need funding.

MOLT could support community services, shared infrastructure and cooperatives that are viable only if customers, operators and funds arrive together. Its first version would enforce money and membership commitments inside one NEAR application. Moving accounts or assets controlled by another platform requires that platform's supported export or integration.

## Existing work shapes both proposals

Blender's community buyout, Unglue.it's Buy-to-Unglue model, time-based source-license conversion, crowdacting and mutual-assurance funding all precede this round. The project opportunity is a useful implementation and a well-chosen first application. Renaming these mechanisms would not establish differentiation. ## Selection judgment

SUNSET has the clearer philosophical claim and a memorable before-and-after demonstration: paying for a product helps make that product available to everyone. Its difficult proof is the release mechanism, especially when its creator is absent.

MOLT is the stronger direction if the most interesting problem is collective agency: giving a group a way to turn separate conditional intentions into one funded decision. Its difficult proof combines a correct agreement engine with a community that actually needs it.
