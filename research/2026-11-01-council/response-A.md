Keep SUNSET as the selected build. Its claim should be a creator-funded public release agreement that survives the operator, demonstrated with an offline learning resource. It is worth building as a mechanism prototype; the research does not yet establish a viable education marketplace.

The strongest result is concrete: a sponsor funds a particular version; escrow pays its creator only after the committed key is disclosed; an independent recipient verifies and uses the released resource; failed disclosure allows the specified refund. NEAR has a substantive role in the shared financial agreement.

SUNSET's originality is limited but defensible at the implementation level. Unglue.it explicitly proposed purchases funding a revenue target for open release in 2013, including the incentive to wait near completion. Its current rights-holder page describes a different support model; the historical proposal does not establish present commercial success. [Original mechanism](https://blog.unglue.it/2013/08/06/a-new-kind-of-ungluing-campaign/), [current guide](https://unglue.it/rightsholders/). Offline educational delivery is also established: Kolibri already supports customized content and offline distribution. [Kolibri](https://learningequality.org/kolibri/about-kolibri/).

The useful difference is therefore the complete, inspectable release/refund and independent reuse workflow, not inventing public buyouts, offline learning, or an AI education platform.

The alternatives compare as follows:

| Candidate | Real potential value | Why I would not switch now |
| --- | --- | --- |
| SUNSET | Make compensation and eventual reuse compatible; reduce dependence on the original operator | Clearest before/after demo; feasible without paid inference or confidential infrastructure |
| COMMONS | Help workers make recurring decisions using sensitive collective evidence | Stronger underlying recurring question, but defensible release-once privacy, runtime compatibility, governance and cohort recruitment remain substantial unresolved gates |
| LIFEBOAT | Let a service owner replace an operator while fencing obsolete monetary authority | Convincing technical demo, but differentiation from existing durable execution, takeover and account-leasing tools needs a narrower proven integration advantage |

No candidate has validated demand. SUNSET's selection is an engineering and demonstration judgment, not evidence that schools or creators will buy it.

The most convincing MVP should contain one original pack, one game, one campaign that releases, one that refunds, and one separate finite support grant. Prioritize the complete proof before building a general marketplace.

A strong three-minute demonstration:

1. Play the free sample immediately, without a wallet.
2. Inspect the committed resource, rights, target and payment split. Complete a real testnet order and inspect actual balances.
3. Stop SUNSET's API and creator process. A different licensed adult publishes the committed key with their own wallet and retained backup.
4. A fresh client verifies the exact resource, exports it, closes the website and completes the game with networking disabled.
5. Show a separate missed-release campaign refund and a support grant that cannot exceed its cap. Copies continue working after its budget expires.

Use actual receipts and artifact hashes. Wrong-key, cap-race and replay attempts are supporting evidence; the memorable outcome is another person using the resource after SUNSET disappears.

The material risks are:

- Pre-acceptance delivery enables strategic refusal. A pending buyer receives the full reusable pack and key, can refuse acceptance, and can recover both portions after expiry. Offline copies cannot be technically revoked. This is disclosed in the design, but it materially limits an adversarial public marketplace. Demonstrate the chosen policy honestly with adult sponsors; do not imply solved fair exchange.
- The sample resource may have little purchasing value. A small integer-maths pack competes with abundant open material. Its purpose is to demonstrate the mechanism. Teacher review alone does not establish willingness to pay or educational differentiation.
- An ordinary commission may be better. A sponsor could pay a creator to publish openly without running a purchase campaign. SUNSET must justify its added steps through shared funding, fixed terms, recoverability and independent fulfillment.
- The creator bears delayed income. Buyout compensation stays escrowed until completion. This is unattractive for creators needing production funding; milestone advances would change the guarantee.
- Release is conditional on honest review and retained bytes. The contract checks a key commitment, not educational quality, rights ownership or successful decryption. At least one competent reviewer and surviving artifact copy remain necessary.
- Track expansion can obscure the product. Encryption is not automatically Privacy; policy scripts are not automatically AI Swarms; a maths game is not automatically a game with a real economy. Extra tracks should follow useful implemented behavior and the current organizer requirements.

The research is unusually candid about these limitations. Preserve that discipline during implementation. SUNSET succeeds as a hackathon build if it proves the complete agreement and recovery path; commercial or educational impact remains a later validation task.

This assessment describes the October design reviewed on 1 November 2026, before the proposed reduction to immediate capped sponsorship. That reduction removes the pending-order fair-exchange issue by changing the product into explicit sponsorship rather than a purchase awaiting acceptance. It should be described consistently in the contract, application and demo.
