# SUNSET v5 — directing specification

Status: production direction locked to the 44-word narration in [SCRIPT.md](SCRIPT.md). The measured male read sets the final 24-second timing of [STORYBOARD.md](STORYBOARD.md). Preserve the operator-approved v4 motion style.

## Narrative contract

- Audience: adult community sponsors, makers and hackathon judges.
- One remembered idea: communities can fund a creator's public learning release on NEAR, then people keep a usable offline copy.
- One visual carrier: the same learning pack, across the complete film.
- The headline must say what the viewer should understand in that beat. A word is not clear merely because it is large.
- No independent NFT explanation, brand-only introduction, transaction tutorial, feature catalogue or long quiz sequence.
- Runtime: exactly 24 seconds at 30fps. Spoken phrases run from 0.200 to 22.328 seconds. The final 1.672 seconds leave space for the result and music credit. Do not stretch narration.

## Voice and sound

The user chose a calm, confident adult male. Use an even lower-register delivery with natural pauses and clear diction. No cartoon inflection, promotional shout, forced excitement or exaggerated upswing.

The selected bed is Scott Buckley's Computations (No Cello), with attribution on the end card after 22.328 seconds and full linked credit in the accompanying post/source documentation. Music should feel restrained and deliberate: a low rhythmic pulse, textured percussion and controlled harmonic movement, with no added sound effects. Its change in weight follows the release and retained-copy payoff. Avoid bright toy-like arpeggios, synthetic chimes/dings, coin showers and a success-game cadence. Do not judge tone from loudness statistics alone. A master can pass technical checks and still fail the user's requested mood; record perceptual listening honestly.

## Colour and material grammar

- Main dark ground: graphite near #101316.
- Main light ground: neutral ivory near #F2F0E9.
- Primary copy: ivory on graphite, or graphite on ivory.
- Muted copper is an accent for selected physical surfaces, one emphasis or a trajectory. It is not the default text colour.
- Use restrained metallic and solid materials; avoid a saturated orange field behind every idea.
- Actual UI keeps its meaningful source colours.
- Before accepting an essential-text hold, test contrast against the actual pixels behind its full glyph area. Target at least 4.5:1 for all essential copy, including large type; moving reflections do not excuse a failed read.
- Protect copy with a solid or controlled neutral text plane when the object surface cannot maintain separation. Do not solve the problem only with outlines, heavy shadows or a full-frame dark wash.

The palette values and 4.5:1 target are production choices for this film, not a claim that every frame has already been measured.

## Typography and read windows

- Main titles: at most five words per line, usually one short phrase at a time.
- Every essential line is stable and unoccluded for at least 0.3 seconds per word plus 0.5 seconds.
- Main-copy height at 1080p: aim for 74–132px. Captions: aim for 48–56px, maximum two lines.
- Final output is intended to remain clear in a 360–390px-wide social player.
- Keep primary text inside the central 80% title-safe area. Captions occupy a reserved lower band; objects and decorative labels cannot intrude there.
- Narration captions match the exact words. Headlines are shorter anchors, not a competing sentence.
- Decorative cropped/scattered letters may remain energetic, but required words must also have a complete readable presentation.
- Do not put essential messages into a rotating 3D texture. Model lettering is secondary; protected graphic text carries the necessary read.
- The final URL holds steady at 21.000–24.000 seconds, exactly three seconds.

## Shot and transition grammar

Retain v4's macro/medium/wide changes, per-glyph motion, image fragments and full-frame hierarchy changes. Each move must lead the eye to a narrative change:

1. The resource survives its host.
2. Communities fund the same resource.
3. A matching key opens its release.
4. The creator can withdraw the agreed credit.
5. The alternate missed-deadline condition returns the sponsor's claim.
6. The released resource becomes a real local copy.
7. The original mark and URL arrive.

Use an object edge, pack face, seam or matched shape as the handoff. Reserve extreme crops and rapid glyph punctuation for connective moments; readable arrivals carry the meaning. A fast move may take 0.4–0.6 seconds, then land. Do not extend arbitrary drifting after the read has settled.

The small persistent testnet identifier and original mark may stay where needed for truthful framing and the already approved visual direction. Generic skill defaults must not reset this film into an unrelated website-card template.

## Review gates

1. Narration: exact 44 words, natural complete phrases, measured boundaries and appropriate male delivery.
2. Story: the narration alone communicates the offer, and the muted pictures communicate the same offer.
3. Read: inspect every essential hold at full size and phone width, including entry/exit and worst-background frames; no clipping or occlusion.
4. Continuity: the same pack carries the action. Every transition has a visible reason.
5. Quality: check the actual native 3D source, text/texture resolution and movement; final container dimensions alone do not establish image detail. Avoid repeated lossy assembly and motion interpolation over model lettering.
6. Final artifact: actual resolution/FPS, complete decode, frame count, exact captions, measured voice overlap/tail, true peak/loudness, source identities and preserved previous version.
7. Fresh eyes: a reviewer gets only the locked narration, board and final review sheets, and reports concrete P0/P1 problems with timestamps.

The hard blockers are unreadable or wrong copy, a misleading product claim, broken motion, visibly soft source detail in a hero shot, or a story that a first-time viewer cannot retell. Technical codec checks alone do not clear those blockers.
