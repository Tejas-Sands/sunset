## The story

One creator makes one useful learning pack. A community funds its public release under clear terms. NEAR holds the sponsorship and enforces the release conditions. The creator can claim their agreed payment; learners keep a usable copy. The film follows that same pack from the work being made to the copy remaining on a device.

**The remembered idea:** pay a creator for making useful learning public, then let people keep the result independently of the platform.

**Audience:** potential sponsors, creators and judges; the learner is the beneficiary. The maths game is an example inside the pack, not the whole identity of SUNSET. Do not present an invented teacher/customer as a real adopter.

**Proposed length:** about 50–60 seconds, aiming near 55. These are editorial estimates, not a final recording clock. Allow a relaxed human read and a genuine action/result hold. Do not force the story back into 24 seconds or stretch a short edit to fill a target.

## Human voiceover draft — for later, not generated

Good learning takes work. But who pays for it when everyone should be able to use it?

SUNSET lets a community fund a pack’s public release.

First, the pack and release terms are reviewed. Sponsors can check the funding goal and deadlines. A NEAR smart contract holds the funds and enforces those rules.

Meet the goal and publish the matching key on time. The pack opens for everyone, and the creator can withdraw the agreed payment.

If funding falls short by the deadline, or release is late, sponsors can claim refunds.

Download a copy. It works offline—even if SUNSET disappears. Learners don’t need a wallet.

SUNSET. Support once. Share forever.

Approximate spoken word count: **111**. This is a reference script for a human recording or the operator’s chosen voice method. No TTS/model/API is part of this draft or the next background-only picture export. The voice should explain an idea to one person, without a commercial-announcer cadence. Time a real read before final voice synchronization.

## Directing — what returns from v3/v4

- Use v4’s changing scale, macro details, foreground passes, individual letter assembly and object-to-object handoffs. A page can become a funding boundary, then an opening pack, then the actual file. The viewer always follows the same resource.
- Compose each beat differently. Enter the next scene through an object already present. Avoid repeated centred headings over diagrams, matching-length chapters and perpetual side dots.
- Keep the warm original mark and legible colour separation. Layered artwork and camera-like movement can create depth; avoid the heavy dark-metal treatment the operator rejected.
- Motion expresses work, gathering, holding, matching, opening, returning and copying. Important words settle and remain readable; entry/exit activity does not count as reading time.
- Give one big visual payoff to the pack opening/public-copy release. Let the real action/result breathe instead of racing past it.
- Strip incidental blockchain labels from the main read. Communicate what the contract does; account/hash detail belongs only where it helps the key/escrow action.

## Background sound only

The picture version will contain a warm rhythmic instrumental bed and a few causal sounds, with no generated narration. Begin with a restrained texture; the groove gains weight as sponsorship gathers, opens at release, lowers for the real action/result, and resolves with the mark. Use a paper movement, a precise latch/contact, a real action click and an outward copy sweep where motivated. Avoid toy chimes, alarms, casino-like coin sounds and a dramatic trailer score. Choose and verify actual licensed recordings after script approval; do not compose a speculative soundtrack in code now.

Keep the human voiceover on a separate future track. Provide room for it and retain a clean background-only export when the video is eventually made. Required music attribution stays in accompanying post/description/source credits, not the end card. Demo/repository links, event wording/tags and a specific feedback invitation remain in the accompanying public post.

## Factual and production boundaries

- Review is performed by the designated reviewer; the smart contract checks commitments and state, not educational quality. Do not imply sponsors can inspect the whole encrypted pack before release.
- Funding target reached plus matching key published on time permits release/creator credit. Actual withdrawal is a separate action.
- Refund claims apply to the relevant missed funding/release condition before successful release. Do not say all payments/refunds happen automatically.
- Once downloaded, a standalone copy can outlast its host. Do not promise permanent hosting or that every resource will always be available to download.
- Learners do not need a wallet for the supported standalone pack. Sponsors and creators still need real account/signing steps for onchain actions.
- Financial object scenes remain labelled illustrations; actual recorded UI remains distinguishable. No settled transaction is rerun for footage, and no independent customer or educator validation is invented.
- The existing real recording is low-rate native screenshot sampling held by true time, not 30 unique browser frames per second. Reuse or recapture later with truthful provenance; this script does not claim a new recording.

## Alternatives considered

1. **One pack, many lives — recommended above.** The funding need comes first; the same resource carries the explanation and payoff.
2. **The website disappears, then rewind.** Start on the surviving offline copy and reveal its funding afterward. Visually striking, but the time jump makes the deal harder to follow.
3. **Sponsor’s decision.** Follow one sponsor from “what am I funding?” through the agreement and release. Clear for funders, but weaker at showing creator work and universal access.
